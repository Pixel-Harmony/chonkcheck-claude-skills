# ChonkCheck Brand Voice Guide

## Core Identity

ChonkCheck is a friend who gets it—conversational, irreverent, food-focused, and anti-diet-culture. We talk like a mate, not a corporation.

---

## Voice Principles

| Principle | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Conversational** | Use contractions, direct address, informal language | "Let's get you sorted" not "Welcome to the application" |
| **Foodie-focused** | Celebrate food pleasure, use British slang | "Scan your scran", "Cook twice? Log once" |
| **Anti-diet culture** | No guilt, no judgment, focus on sustainability | "Watch the trend, not the wobble" |
| **Benefits-first** | Describe outcomes, not features | "Point your camera at any barcode" not "Barcode scanning API" |
| **Short & punchy** | 8-15 word sentences, active voice | "Recipe saved! Nice work" |
| **British English** | British spellings, slang, metric units | colour, centre, scran, sorted, kg |

---

## Tone by Context

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| Marketing | Playful, bold, benefit-driven | "Spend less time logging, more time eating." |
| App UI | Friendly, clear, efficient | "No foods found. Add one to get started!" |
| Errors | Helpful, never punishing | "Oops! That doesn't look right. Try a value between 1 and 300?" |
| Success | Encouraging, not cringy | "Recipe saved! Nice work" |

---

## Words We Use

**Food & tracking**: scran, chonk, macros, diary, sorted, track, add, save, scan, snap, tap

**Positive language**: sorted, nice work, looking good, on a roll, get started

**British**: colour, centre, flavour, realise, customise, mobile (not cell phone), kg, cm, £

---

## Words We Avoid

| Category | Avoid | Use Instead |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| Diet culture | diet, cheat meal, guilt, naughty, clean eating | tracking, goals, meals |
| Corporate | utilize, facilitate, optimize, solution, comprehensive | use, help, improve, specific benefit |
| Generic | innovative, revolutionary, cutting-edge | show the capability |
| User-blaming | invalid, error, failed, incorrect | "Oops! That doesn't look right..." |

## Wordplay Guidelines

**Use puns in**: Marketing headlines, playful CTAs ("See what's cooking")

**Don't use in**: Errors, form labels, critical instructions

**Good**: "Scan your scran" (rhymes, British, fun)
**Bad**: "The whey to success" (too niche, unclear)

**Rule**: If the pun makes the message less clear, cut it.
